# Shiplog brand book

Oct 23, 2026 · @Khushi Mehta

How Shiplog looks, sounds and explains itself, for the website, decks, emails, LinkedIn and product.

## Brand foundations

Shiplog is a personalisation company. We make personalised customer engagement no code, beautiful and easy for every team, built on deep customer intelligence, and we take the action instead of stopping at a prediction. Everything we write and design should make that feel simple, specific and inevitable.

**Mission:** Make personalisation the default, not a luxury, for every company.

**Vision:** Shiplog becomes the layer every company uses to understand each customer and act on it.

**The belief behind it:** Engineering got industrialised. Pricing got individualised. Customer engagement got stuck in the middle, with static segments and calendar campaigns. Agents that reason about each customer replace them.

**Positioning statement:** For growth and customer teams who want every customer to get the right message at the right moment, Shiplog is the no code personalisation platform, built on deep customer intelligence, that finds the next best action for each customer and takes it. Unlike prediction tools that stop at a score, Shiplog does the work, beautifully, with the whole team in one place.

**Our two differentiators**

1. **Actions, not predictions.** Churn scores and health dashboards tell you who might leave. Shiplog decides what to do, builds the message and sends it, with your approval.
2. **Multiplayer.** Growth, CS, product and sales work on the same customers in one place, instead of passing spreadsheets between teams.

**What we research:** next best action. We treat choosing the right action for each customer as an open research problem and publish what we learn.

**What powers it: deep customer intelligence.** A live profile of every customer, built from product, billing, CRM and support data, that gets sharper with every message and reply. It covers every account, not a sample, and every insight shows the signals behind it. This is what makes our actions the right ones, and it is our main focus for enterprise.

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| A personalisation company | Another email tool or CDP |
| Actions you can send today | Predictions and scores someone still has to act on |
| Multiplayer: the whole team in one place | A single user tool that creates hand offs |
| No code, beautiful and easy | A platform that needs engineers to operate |
| Agents that propose, with humans who approve | A black box that sends on its own |
| Specific, numerate, calm | Hype, buzzwords, "revolutionary" |

**Brand personality:** the sharp operator friend. Knows the numbers, tells you the truth kindly, and helps you do the thing rather than talk about it.

## Voice and tone

Shiplog sounds like a sharp operator who respects your time: confident without hype, warm without being soft, and always specific.

**Four voice principles**

1. **Specific beats impressive.** "10 plan upgrades" beats "significant expansion". Names, numbers, dates.
2. **Direct and kind.** Say the hard thing clearly, then help. Never cold, never fluffy.
3. **Dry wit over enthusiasm.** A light line ("This page churned.") is welcome. Exclamation marks and "excited to announce" are not.
4. **Write like you talk.** Short sentences, plain words, active voice. If you wouldn't say it out loud, rewrite it.

**Tone by context**

| Context | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Calm, confident, visual | Know what every customer needs next. Then do it. |
| Cold outreach | Short, specific, one ask | Saw you launched usage based pricing. That usually creates an upgrade moment nobody owns. Worth a look? |
| LinkedIn | Observation, insight, evidence | Most PLG revenue is lost in the first 30 days, not at renewal. Here's what we've seen. |
| Product UI | Clear, quiet, helpful | 142 accounts match. Review before sending. |
| Customer emails (for clients) | The client's voice, never ours | Built from their website and brand kit |
| Bad news | Own it, explain it, fix it | We sent a campaign to the wrong segment. Here's what happened and what we changed. |
| Investors | Headline, good, bad, ask | Revenue grew \[x\]% this month. Activation is slower than planned. We need intros to CS leaders. |

## Writing rules and vocabulary

Ten rules cover almost every case. When in doubt, cut a word and add a number.

**Rules**

1. No dashes of any kind. Use a full stop, a comma or "and".
2. British English across the site: personalisation, behaviour, optimise.
3. Numbers as digits (5, not five) except at the start of a sentence.
4. Customers are never named in public material.
5. One idea per sentence, one point per paragraph.
6. Lead with the outcome, then explain how.
7. Headlines are sentences with a full stop, in sentence case.
8. Buttons start with a verb: Book a demo, Get your audit, Request access.
9. No exclamation marks, no emoji in product or website copy.
10. Every claim has a number or an example behind it.

**Say this, not that**

| Say | Not |
| --- | --- |
| Personalisation | Hyper personalisation, 1:1 marketing magic |
| Actions, not predictions | Propensity scores, predictive insights |
| Multiplayer, the whole team | Hand offs, single user workflows |
| No code, beautiful, easy | Low code, powerful, robust |
| Next best action | Smart recommendations, AI magic |
| Agents propose, your team approves | Fully autonomous, set and forget |
| Every customer | Every segment, personas |
| Revenue already in your customer base | Untapped potential, unlock value |
| Works with your stack | Rip and replace, all in one platform |
| Built, shipped, found | Leveraged, empowered, enabled |

**Words we never use:** revolutionary, game changing, cutting edge, best in class, seamless, synergy, unlock, leverage, thrilled, delighted.

## Colour

A quiet, near monochrome base with one signal colour. The signal colour marks the customer's path and the main action, nothing else. This palette is a proposal: swap in your current brand values if you already have them.

| Name | Light mode | Dark mode | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | #111318 | #F4F4F1 | Headlines and body text |
| Paper | #FBFAF7 | #0E1014 | Page background |
| Slate | #5B6170 | #A3A8B4 | Secondary text, captions |
| Mist | #EEF0F4 | #1A1D24 | Cards, section backgrounds |
| Line | #D9DCE3 | #2A2E37 | Borders, dividers, diagram connectors |
| Signal | #3B5BFD | #6E86FF | Primary buttons, the customer path in diagrams, links |
| Signal tint | #E8ECFF | #1C2347 | Highlighted boxes, selected states |
| Growth | #1F9D6B | #3CC48D | Positive results, upgrades, success states |
| Amber | #E8A33D | #F0B85E | At risk signals, warnings |
| Coral | #D9534F | #EF7A76 | Churn, errors, leaks in diagrams |

**Usage rules**

- Signal appears at most once or twice per screen: the main button and the customer path.
- Growth, Amber and Coral only carry meaning (result, risk, leak). Never decoration.
- Text on Signal is white; check every pair against WCAG AA contrast.
- No gradients or shadows on product visuals. Flat colour keeps diagrams readable.

## Typography, layout and shape

Two typefaces, one grid, soft corners. The system should feel engineered and calm, never busy.

**Typefaces**

- **Geist** (or Inter as a fallback) for headlines, body and UI.
- **Geist Mono** (or JetBrains Mono) for numbers, signals, labels and code. Using mono for data makes proof points feel measured.

**Type scale (desktop / mobile, in px)**

| Style | Size | Weight | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | 64 / 40 | 600 | Homepage H1 only |
| H1 | 48 / 34 | 600 | Page titles |
| H2 | 32 / 26 | 600 | Section headlines |
| H3 | 22 / 20 | 600 | Card titles |
| Body large | 20 / 18 | 400 | Hero subheads |
| Body | 17 / 16 | 400 | Paragraphs |
| Label (mono) | 13 / 12 | 500 | Eyebrows, signals, captions |
| Big number (mono) | 56 / 40 | 500 | Proof stats |

Headlines use tight letter spacing (minus 2%). Body line height is 1.6, headlines 1.15.

**Layout**

- 12 column grid, 1200 px max content width, 24 px gutters.
- Spacing on an 8 px scale: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128.
- Sections are separated by 128 px on desktop, 80 px on mobile.
- One idea and one visual per section.

**Shape**

- Corner radius: 8 px for buttons and inputs, 16 px for cards, 24 px for large panels.
- Borders 1 px in Line colour. No drop shadows; use Mist backgrounds to separate surfaces.
- Buttons: Signal fill with white text for primary; Ink outline for secondary.

## Diagrams, illustration, motion and imagery

Diagrams are Shiplog's signature. They show customers moving from data to decision to action, and they should be recognisable at a glance on any slide or page.

**Diagram system**

- **Flow direction:** always left to right. Data sources on the left, Shiplog in the centre, channels and outcomes on the right.
- **Customers are dots.** A single customer is a small Signal coloured dot; groups are clusters of dots, never person icons.
- **Shiplog is a layer.** Draw it as a wide rounded band in Signal tint, not a logo in a box.
- **Tools are logos** in neutral boxes with Line borders.
- **Meaning colours:** Growth for upgrades and wins, Amber for risk, Coral for leaks. Everything else stays Ink, Slate and Line.
- **Labels** in Geist Mono, 12 to 13 px, sentence case.

**Illustration**

No characters, mascots or 3D blobs. Illustrations are abstract and diagrammatic: dots, paths, layers and journey maps. If an image doesn't explain something, cut it.

**Motion**

- Motion explains flow: dots travel along paths, layers light up when a decision happens.
- Durations 200 to 400 ms for UI, up to 1.2 s for diagram sequences. Ease out.
- Animations play once on scroll; the hero stream may loop slowly.
- Always respect reduced motion settings with a static version.

**Imagery**

- Product screenshots: real UI on a Mist background, cropped to the part that matters, with one Signal highlight.
- People photos only for the team and events, natural light, no stock photography.
- Customer logos never appear, since customers stay anonymous.
