How Shiplog looks, sounds and explains itself, for the website, decks, emails, LinkedIn and product.

## Brand foundations

Shiplog is a personalisation company built for growth teams. We make personalised customer engagement no code, beautiful and easy for every team, built on deep customer intelligence, and we take the action instead of stopping at a prediction. Everything we write and design should make that feel simple, specific and inevitable.

**The one line:** Personalisation your growth team can run on its own. No engineers. No data team. No design team.

**Mission:** Make personalisation the default for every company, run by the growth team alone.

**Vision:** Shiplog becomes the layer every company uses to understand each customer and act on it.

**The belief behind it:** Engineering got industrialised. Pricing got individualised. Customer engagement got stuck in the middle, with static segments and calendar campaigns. Agents that reason about each customer replace them.

**Positioning statement:** For growth teams who want every customer to get the right message at the right moment, without waiting on engineers, data or design, Shiplog is the no code personalisation platform, built on deep customer intelligence, that finds the next best action for each customer and takes it. Unlike prediction tools that stop at a score, Shiplog does the work, beautifully, with the whole team in one place.

**Our two differentiators**

1. **Actions, not predictions.** Churn scores and health dashboards tell you who might leave. Shiplog decides what to do, builds the message and sends it, with your approval.
2. **Multiplayer.** Growth, CS, product and sales work on the same customers in one place, instead of passing spreadsheets between teams.

**What we research:** next best action. We treat choosing the right action for each customer as an open research problem and publish what we learn.

**What powers it: deep customer intelligence.** A live profile of every customer, built from product, billing, CRM and support data, that gets sharper with every message and reply. It covers every account, not a sample, and every insight shows the signals behind it. This is what makes our actions the right ones, and it is our main focus for enterprise.

**Built for growth teams to execute.** The buyer and the user is the growth team. Everything we say should make one growth marketer feel they can do this alone.

| Engineers | Tickets to add tracking and build in app messages | Connect tools in a few clicks; in app ready to go |
| Data team | SQL, segments and models before anything sends | Agents decide who gets what, every day |

| --- | --- |

**Brand personality:** the sharp operator friend. Knows the numbers, tells you the truth kindly, and helps you do the thing rather than talk about it.

## Voice and tone

Shiplog sounds like a sharp operator who respects your time: confident without hype, warm without being soft, and always specific.

**Four voice principles**

1. **Specific beats impressive.** "10 plan upgrades" beats "significant expansion". Names, numbers, dates.
2. **Direct and kind.** Say the hard thing clearly, then help. Never cold, never fluffy.
3. **Dry wit over enthusiasm.** A light line ("This page churned.") is welcome. Exclamation marks and "excited to announce" are not.
4. **Write like you talk.** Short sentences, plain words, active voice. If you wouldn't say it out loud, rewrite it.

**Tone by context**

| Context | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Calm, confident, visual | Personalisation your growth team can run on its own. |
| Cold outreach | Short, specific, one ask | Saw you launched usage based pricing. That usually creates an upgrade moment nobody owns. Worth a look? |
| LinkedIn | Observation, insight, evidence | Most PLG revenue is lost in the first 30 days, not at renewal. Here's what we've seen. |
| Product UI | Clear, quiet, helpful | 142 accounts match. Review before sending. |
| Customer emails (for clients) | The client's voice, never ours | Built from their website and brand kit |
| Bad news | Own it, explain it, fix it | We sent a campaign to the wrong segment. Here's what happened and what we changed. |
| Investors | Headline, good, bad, ask | Revenue grew \[x\]% this month. Activation is slower than planned. We need intros to CS leaders. |

## Writing rules and vocabulary

Ten rules cover almost every case. When in doubt, cut a word and add a number.

**Rules**

1. No dashes of any kind. Use a full stop, a comma or "and".
2. British English across the site: personalisation, behaviour, optimise.
3. Numbers as digits (5, not five) except at the start of a sentence.
4. Customers are never named in public material.
5. One idea per sentence, one point per paragraph.
6. Lead with the outcome, then explain how.
7. Headlines are sentences with a full stop, in sentence case.
8. Buttons start with a verb: Book a demo, Get your audit, Request access.
9. No exclamation marks, no emoji in product or website copy.
10. Every claim has a number or an example behind it.

**Say this, not that**

| Say | Not |
| --- | --- |
| Your growth team runs it | Requires engineering resources, cross functional rollout |
| No engineers, no data team, no design team | Low code, minimal setup |
| Personalisation | Hyper personalisation, 1:1 marketing magic |
| Actions, not predictions | Propensity scores, predictive insights |
| Multiplayer, the whole team | Hand offs, single user workflows |
| No code, beautiful, easy | Powerful, robust, enterprise grade |
| Next best action | Smart recommendations, AI magic |
| Agents propose, your team approves | Fully autonomous, set and forget |
| Every customer | Every segment, personas |
| Revenue already in your customer base | Untapped potential, unlock value |
| Works with your stack | Rip and replace, all in one platform |
| Built, shipped, found | Leveraged, empowered, enabled |

**Words we never use:** revolutionary, game changing, cutting edge, best in class, seamless, synergy, unlock, leverage, thrilled, delighted.

## Visual identity: the bloom

The whole identity grows from one shape: four petals meeting at a single centre. The customer sits in the middle; the petals are the four moves Shiplog makes around them: understand, decide, act, learn.

The shape says what the product feels like. It is soft and round, so it feels easy. It is perfectly symmetrical, so it feels engineered. It opens outward, so it feels like growth. Every visual choice below comes from it: rounded corners, quarter circle curves, four accent colours and a loader that blooms.

**Four words to design by:** Easy · Fast · Precise · Warm. If a page feels heavy, slow, vague or cold, it is off brand.

## Logo

The logo has two parts: the symbol (the bloom) and the wordmark. Use the horizontal lockup by default and the symbol alone when space is tight or the brand is already clear.

&#91;image: Shiplog primary lockup\]

&#91;image: Shiplog symbol\]

**Construction**

- Each petal is a lens drawn from two quarter circles, centred on the corners of its square. The outer tip has a small rounded corner, and the petals meet at the centre with a hairline gap.
- In the lockup, the space between symbol and wordmark is a quarter of the symbol's width. The wordmark sits centred on the symbol's middle line.

**Clear space and size**

- Clear space on every side: a quarter of the symbol's width (call it x). Nothing enters it.
- Minimum size on screen: lockup 88 px wide, symbol 16 px. In print: lockup 20 mm, symbol 6 mm.

**Colourways**

&#91;embedded content: Shiplog symbol colourways · 4 approved versions\]

Ink on Paper is the logo in 90% of uses. The four colour bloom is a special edition for the app icon, social avatars and event moments; never use it next to the wordmark on a page.

**Never**

- Rotate, stretch or skew the symbol (rotated, it reads as an X).
- Recolour petals outside the bloom edition, outline the logo, or add shadows, glows or gradients to the lockup.
- Retype the wordmark in another font, or move the symbol to the right of it.
- Place the logo on busy photos or low contrast colours.

## Colour

A warm, light, almost monochrome base with four bright petal colours used in small doses. The rule is 80 / 15 / 5: 80% light neutrals, 15% Ink, 5% bloom colour.

**Neutrals**

| Stone | #6E6E78 | Secondary text, captions |

**Bloom colours** (one per petal, one per stage of the loop)

| Name | Hex | Tint | Stage | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sky | #4DA3FF | #EAF4FF | Understand | Signals, intelligence, data |
| Iris | #6B5CFF | #EFEDFF | Decide | The main accent: links, focus rings, selected states, the customer path |
| Coral | #FF6A3D | #FFEFEA | Act | Messages sent, campaign moments |
| Mint | #2EC4A0 | #E6F8F3 | Learn | Results, growth, success |

**Bloom gradient:** Sky to Iris to Coral, at 120 degrees. For brand moments only: the app icon, the hero glow, social cards. Once per page at most.

**Status colours:** success uses Mint text #0E8F6E, warning Amber #E59A1E, error Red #E5484D.

**Rules**

- Primary buttons are Ink pills with white text. Elegant, high contrast, and never competing with the content.
- Iris is the only bloom colour allowed for text (links). Sky, Coral and Mint are fills and tints only.
- In diagrams, each stage keeps its petal colour everywhere: a Coral box always means an action.

## Icons

One outline icon set, used sparingly: icons label concepts, they never decorate. Use [Lucide](https://lucide.dev) so the site, product and decks share one set.

**Style rules**

- Outline only, 1.5 px stroke, rounded ends, on a 24 px grid. No filled, duotone or 3D icons.
- Colour: Ink by default, Stone when secondary, Iris only for the active or selected state. Rounded joins echo the bloom.
- Sizes: 16 px in UI and tables, 20 px in body text, 24 px in feature blocks, 32 px maximum on marketing pages.
- Always paired with a word. An icon never carries meaning on its own.

**Core concept icons**

| A signal | activity | Signals in use case tabs and profiles |

**Don't:** mix icon sets, use emoji in product or website copy, or put icons on every bullet.

## Typography, layout and shape

One clean sans for almost everything, one elegant serif italic for a single moment of warmth, one mono for data. All three are free.

**Typefaces**

| Role | Typeface | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display and UI | Geist: Semibold 600, Medium 500, Regular 400 | Crisp and technical; sits next to the heavy wordmark without competing |
| Accent | Instrument Serif, Italic | One phrase per headline, for warmth and elegance |
| Data | Geist Mono, Medium 500 | Numbers, signals, labels, code |

Example headline: Personalisation your growth team can run *on its own*. The sans carries the message; the serif italic carries the feeling.

**Type scale (desktop / mobile, px)**

| Style | Size | Weight | Tracking | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | 72 / 44 | 600 | minus 3.5% | Homepage hero only |
| H1 | 52 / 36 | 600 | minus 3% | Page titles |
| H2 | 36 / 28 | 600 | minus 2% | Section headlines |
| H3 | 22 / 20 | 600 | minus 1% | Card titles |
| Body large | 20 / 18 | 400 | 0 | Hero subheads |
| Body | 16 / 16 | 400 | 0 | Paragraphs, line height 1.6 |
| Small | 14 / 14 | 400 | 0 | Captions, helper text |
| Label (mono) | 12 / 12 | 500 | plus 2% | Eyebrows, signals, tags |
| Big number (mono) | 64 / 44 | 500 | minus 2% | Proof stats |

**Type rules:** sentence case everywhere; at most one serif phrase per headline and never in body text; lines no longer than 64 characters; headlines in Ink, body in Graphite.

**Layout**

- 12 column grid, 1200 px max content width, 24 px gutters.
- Spacing on an 8 px scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 160.
- Generous white space: 160 px between sections on desktop, 96 px on mobile. When in doubt, add space, not elements.
- One idea and one visual per section.

**Shape (taken from the petal tips)**

- Buttons and tags are pills. Inputs 12 px radius, cards 20 px, large panels and screenshots 32 px.
- Borders are 1 px Line.
- One soft shadow, for floating elements only (cards over content, menus, modals): 0 1px 2px rgba(11,11,12,0.04), 0 12px 32px rgba(11,11,12,0.06).
- Primary button: Ink pill, white text, 44 px tall. Secondary: White pill with a Line border and Ink text. Text link: Iris.

## Graphics, diagrams and motion

Diagrams and motion carry the feeling of speed and ease. They should look like the product is already working.

**Diagram system**

- Flow runs left to right: data sources, then Shiplog, then channels and results.
- Shiplog is drawn as the bloom symbol at the centre, never as a generic box.
- Each stage keeps its petal colour: Sky for signals, Iris for decisions, Coral for actions, Mint for results.
- Customers are small Iris dots; groups are clusters of dots, never person icons.
- Containers are White cards with 20 px corners and the soft shadow; connectors are 1.5 px Line with rounded ends.
- Labels in Geist Mono, 12 px, Stone.

**Graphic devices**

- **The quarter circle.** Taken from the petals. Use it to round one corner of a screenshot more than the others, or as a large soft shape behind product visuals.
- **Petal tints.** Big, soft tint shapes (Sky, Iris, Coral or Mint tint) behind product shots, one colour per section.
- **Dot grid.** A faint dot pattern at 4% Ink behind diagrams, suggesting customers and data.

**Motion**

- Speed is the feeling: UI transitions run in 150 to 250 ms with the curve cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1).
- Sections fade up 12 px as they enter, once.
- **The bloom loader:** the four petals light up clockwise in Sky, Iris, Coral, Mint on an 800 ms loop. It replaces every spinner.
- Diagrams build from the centre outward, like the bloom opening.
- Reduced motion settings get a static version of everything.

**Imagery**

- Product screenshots sit in White cards with 32 px corners and the soft shadow, on Mist or a petal tint.
- People photos only for the team and events: natural light, real places, no stock.
- No 3D blobs, robots, glowing brains or other AI clichés. No customer logos.
